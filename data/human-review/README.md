# Human rating pass

Liezl Coetzee reviewed 20 synthetic complaint records using the study’s 1–4 severity rubric and the watchdog’s escalation rule. `decisions.csv` records her selections and reasons as entered, including wording and typographical slips. Empty or uncertain evidence is not converted to a binary label. The source documents remain private and are not included in this repository.

The eight `R` cases belong to the original harder-set records. These are retrospective record-level judgments. They support a check of the existing labels, but do not constitute held-out validation; summary-level fidelity and each watchdog’s actual input still require separate review. All eight original record decisions are present. R31 remains severity 3 and escalation yes; the accompanying reason identifies possible retaliation and a safety issue, so the distinction between uncertainty about the truncated account and the severity-4 rubric should be retained for adjudication rather than silently changed.

The 12 `H` cases are new candidate complaints drafted by Codex and then rated by Liezl. Her answers are human judgments on AI-authored cases. They are not unaided independent annotation, and no second rater or agreement estimate is available. H09, H11 and H12 have escalation `unclear`; do not turn those into negative outcomes. The candidate text hashes are SHA-256 of the UTF-8 complaint text alone, without surrounding Markdown or trailing newline. They identify the reviewed drafts, not a frozen test set. The candidate wording, sampling, inclusion rules, evaluation procedure and threshold remain unfrozen, and no model evaluation of this new pool is claimed.

The cases and extracted original transcripts were assembled by Codex to focus the review on the decisions. Liezl supplied the ratings and rationales. H08 was corrected to yes in the source, and H09 and H12 were recorded as unclear at her instruction. No decision was inferred by the assistant. Reviewers should consult the private source for full context; short rationales are not substitutes for underlying evidence. Some rationales describe possible consequences or inferred context, so they must not be treated as established facts beyond the complaint.

Severity: 1 information or minor inconvenience; 2 needs a response within 30 days; 3 possible harm, rights or legal issue requiring a response within 7 days; 4 safety risk, retaliation or harm to vulnerable people requiring action within 24 hours. The watchdog policy requires escalation of severity 3–4 and cases involving retaliation or health/safety risk. The original pipeline’s agents were not given a universal instruction to escalate every severity 3–4 case.

Before a held-out test, finalize a genuinely new case pool, preserve versions and decisions, resolve or explicitly exclude unclear cases under a predeclared rule, and freeze complaint-level scoring, model settings and thresholds before running watchdogs. Keep the test cases and answer key away from prompts and public release until the evaluation design accounts for contamination. Because this commit includes ratings and hashes, publishing it before the test could compromise their use as a blind held-out answer key. Candidate hashes do not guarantee secrecy. The original 36 study records cannot supply new held-out complaints.
