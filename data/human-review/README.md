# Human rating pass

Liezl Coetzee reviewed 20 synthetic complaint records using the study’s 1–4 severity rubric and the watchdog’s escalation rule. `decisions.csv` records her selections and reasons as entered, including wording and typographical slips. Empty or uncertain evidence is not converted to a binary label. Full source documents remain private and are not included in this repository. The public CSV includes short human-written rationales, some of which describe synthetic complaint content; the release omits full complaint texts rather than all case descriptions.

The eight `R` cases belong to the original harder-set records. These are retrospective record-level judgments. They support a check of the existing labels, but do not constitute held-out validation; summary-level fidelity and each watchdog’s actual input still require separate review. All eight original record decisions are present. R31 remains severity 3 and escalation yes; the accompanying reason identifies possible retaliation and a safety issue, so the distinction between uncertainty about the truncated account and the severity-4 rubric should be retained for adjudication rather than silently changed.

H01–H12 are exposed development or practice cases drafted by Codex and then rated by Liezl. Their ratings, reasons and text hashes were published on the public PR branch before testing, so these cases are retired from use as a blind held-out test. Removing the rows or rewriting the branch would not restore that status. Her answers are human judgments on AI-authored cases. Codex’s authorship of the candidate text is separate from Liezl’s rating judgments. No second rater or agreement estimate is available. H09, H11 and H12 have escalation `unclear`; do not turn those into negative outcomes. The candidate text hashes are SHA-256 of the UTF-8 complaint text alone, without surrounding Markdown or trailing newline. They identify the reviewed drafts, not a frozen test set. The candidate wording, sampling, inclusion rules, evaluation procedure and threshold remain unfrozen, and no model evaluation of this new pool is claimed.

AI assistance for the human rating pass was limited to preparing the review pack and capture sheets, bringing the evidence and decision fields together so Liezl did not need to search the datasets or design the capture sheets. Liezl made all ratings and escalation decisions and wrote the reasons. Codex separately authored the 12 new candidate complaints; those texts were then rated by Liezl. H08 was corrected to yes in the source, and H09 and H12 were recorded as unclear at her instruction. No decision was inferred by the assistant. Reviewers should consult the private source for full context; short rationales are not substitutes for underlying evidence. Some rationales describe possible consequences or inferred context, so they must not be treated as established facts beyond the complaint.

Severity: 1 information or minor inconvenience; 2 needs a response within 30 days; 3 possible harm, rights or legal issue requiring a response within 7 days; 4 safety risk, retaliation or harm to vulnerable people requiring action within 24 hours. The watchdog policy requires escalation of severity 3–4 and cases involving retaliation or health/safety risk. The original pipeline’s agents were not given a universal instruction to escalate every severity 3–4 case.

A future held-out test requires a fresh case pool, with its ratings kept off GitHub until the evaluation is frozen and run. Preserve the private versions and decisions, resolve or explicitly exclude unclear cases under a predeclared rule, and freeze complaint-level scoring, model settings and thresholds before running watchdogs. Keep the cases and answer key out of calibration prompts. H01–H12 remain useful for development or practice; candidate hashes do not guarantee secrecy. The original 36 study records cannot supply new held-out complaints.
